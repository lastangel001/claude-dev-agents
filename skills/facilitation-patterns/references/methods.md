# Method catalog — when to use, how to run

Pick by **situation type** and the **pyramid level**. Alternate formats (whole group ↔ pairs ↔
small groups ↔ presentation ↔ voting) to keep energy and avoid groupthink.

## Situation type → method shortlist

| Type | When | Methods |
|------|------|---------|
| **Analysis** (анализ) | Members hold fragments of info; lots of unstructured/incomplete info; need a shared picture | brainstorm, metaphor, skeptics & optimists, SWOT/SOAR, problem tree, Ishikawa, moderation |
| **Options** (варианты) | A clearly stated problem; minimum info to decide is present | brainstorm, brainwriting 6-3-5, 1-2-4-All, "how might it be", moderation, World Café, Liberating Structures |
| **Choice** (выбор) | A list of options exists; pick the best | voting (hands/stickers/dots), sociometry, "if not my idea, then…", criteria-vs-solutions matrix |

## Analysis & generation methods

**Brainstorm (мозговой штурм)** — *when:* summarize what's known + find fresh ideas. *Rules:*
no criticism; one at a time; listen; record everything; no "bad" ideas; enforce rules
immediately; keep a high tempo. *Caveat:* verbal brainstorm lets the loudest dominate and
silences the reserved — the best ideas may never be heard. Prefer 6-3-5 / 1-2-4-All when
inclusion matters.

**Brainwriting 6-3-5** — *when:* quickly collect many options with equal participation; mixed/
cross-unit groups; avoid emotional charge and dominance. *How:* 6 people, 5 minutes, each writes
3 ideas on a sheet; sheets pass to the next person who adds 3 more or builds on existing ones;
repeat several rounds. Parallel, written, no verbal sparring. *Trade-off:* needs paper/pens, less
conversational.

**1-2-4-All** — *when:* engage everyone, mature ideas before open debate; great to open a large
discussion. *How:* solo reflection (1) → share in pairs (2) → pairs merge into fours and
synthesize (4) → fours feed the whole group (All). *Trade-off:* takes longer than direct debate.

**Metaphor (метафорический способ)** — *when:* group is stuck/tired; need fresh, non-standard
angles; surface emotional attitude. *How:* represent the problem as a metaphor — "draw the
problem", "draw the ideal manager", "picture the company as a tree — roots/branches/trunk?".

**"How might it be" (как это могло бы быть)** — *when:* stuck; obvious solutions exhausted, need
a non-standard one. *How:* widen the frame — "How is this solved in other countries/companies?
How was it solved in the past / how would people of the future solve it? How would we solve it
with unlimited resources?"

**Skeptics & optimists (скептики и оптимисты)** — *when:* drain negativity before a topic;
surface pessimistic and optimistic scenarios. *How:* split into two groups; optimists list the
gains/why we can't do without this; skeptics list the losses/negative consequences. A variant of
role discussion ("manager"/"subordinate", etc.).

**SWOT** — *when:* comprehensively review strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats. Works
at base level for fast diagnosis and at strategy level; combine with the Business Model Canvas
for a stronger platform. **SOAR** (Strengths, Opportunities, Aspirations, Results) is an
appreciative, future-oriented alternative when you want energy and ambition over threat-focus.

**Problem tree (дерево проблем)** — *when:* forecast near and far consequences of problems and
assess likelihood. *Rules:* don't skip links in the logical chain; hunt for non-obvious
"branches". Layout: situation → problems → most significant problem → consequences.

**Ishikawa (fishbone)** — *when:* think comprehensively about near and far causes, find the root
cause. *How:* draw the fishbone, problem in the head, categories as bones (People, Methods,
Machines, Materials, Environment), then ask "why?" repeatedly from the most likely category.

**Moderation (модерация)** — *when:* everyone should contribute confidentially; surface
content classes of problems/ideas; let people work solo first to avoid group effects. *How:*
hand out stickers (2–3 colors to mark aspects, or one color); each writes ideas individually,
one per sticker; then group the stickers — in subgroups with ranking, by predefined solution
areas, or by the facilitator clustering "A / B / new group?" one sticker at a time.

## Large-group methods

**World Café** — *when:* large groups (12–30), deep multi-faceted organic discussion of important
questions. *How:* small tables of 4–6; each discusses one prepared question; a host/scribe
records key ideas on a large sheet; every ~20–25 min participants rotate to new tables (the host
stays and briefs newcomers). Ideas cross-pollinate and mature. *Success factors:* a carefully
sequenced set of questions (general → concrete) and a genuine café atmosphere. Has an online
variant (breakout rooms + shared boards).

**Liberating Structures (LS)** — *when:* you want a combinable kit rather than one technique. A
set of ~33 micro-structures, each a precise design for interaction, making work more inclusive,
fast and productive. 1-2-4-All is one of them; **Troika Consultations** lets a person get
feedback on their problem from two others in a short slot. Use the matching tool at
liberatingstructures.info to pick a structure by goal/group size — avoid the paradox of choice.

## Choice / decision methods

**Voting** — *when:* tally support; vary the form (hands / stickers / dots / checkmarks) so the
group doesn't tire of one format. **Sociometry:** hand on the shoulder of whose view you most
agree (or disagree) with; line up 0–10 on a scale; place yourself on an X/Y plane of two
factors; observe which options win and which clusters form.

**"If not my idea, then…" (если не моё решение, то…)** — *when:* people cling to their own
proposal. *How:* each names which option *other than their own* they'd choose; groups pick from
lists made by other groups.

**Criteria-vs-solutions matrix (таблица соответствия)** — *when:* the decision must meet a set
of criteria. *How:* solutions down the first column, criteria across the top; mark mandatory (о)
vs desirable (ж); fill each cell (+) meets / (−) doesn't.

## Comparison cheat-sheet (generation methods)

| Method | Strength | Weakness |
|--------|----------|----------|
| Brainstorm (verbal) | Simple, fast | Loud voices dominate; quiet ones suppressed; quality dips |
| Brainwriting 6-3-5 | Equal participation, parallel, low emotional pressure | Needs materials; less conversational |
| 1-2-4-All | Everyone engaged, ideas mature gradually, lowers fear of speaking | Slower than direct debate |
| SWOT (base) | Structures thinking, fast key-factor surfacing, universal | Can be shallow; risk of mis-classifying factors |
