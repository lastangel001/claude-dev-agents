# Extended toolkit — beyond the core canon

This file develops the direction further: well-established formats and structures that
complement the core method catalog, plus online facilitation and the international ↔ local
synthesis. Use them on top of the foundations in `facilitation-101.md` and `methods.md`.

## ORID / Focused Conversation

A four-level questioning flow that walks a group from data to decision without skipping ground:
- **O — Objective:** facts. "What did we actually see/hear? What happened?"
- **R — Reflective:** reactions. "What surprised, excited, worried you?"
- **I — Interpretive:** meaning. "What does this tell us? What patterns/options?"
- **D — Decisional:** resolve. "What do we decide / do next?"
Great as the backbone of a debrief, a retro, or a feedback session. Maps cleanly onto the goals
pyramid (O/R ≈ shared field, I ≈ alternatives, D ≈ decision/roadmap).

## Decision & prioritization micro-tools

- **Dot-voting (multi-voting):** each person gets N dots to place on options; fast convergence;
  vary placement rules (all on one vs spread) to shape behavior.
- **Fist-to-five / Roman voting (thumbs up/side/down):** quick consensus check; anyone below "3
  fingers" speaks to what would raise their support — surfaces concerns before committing.
- **Impact/Effort (2×2):** plot options to find quick wins vs big bets vs fill-ins vs money pits.
- **Criteria matrix:** see `methods.md`. Pair with dot-voting for shortlist → score.

## Retrospective formats

- **Start / Stop / Continue** — simplest action-oriented retro.
- **Mad / Sad / Glad** — surfaces emotion before action; good when morale is a factor.
- **4 Ls (Liked / Learned / Lacked / Longed for)** — balanced reflection.
- **Sailboat / Speedboat** — wind (helps), anchors (slows), rocks (risks), island (goal) —
  metaphor-driven, energizing. Always close a retro with owned next steps (the after-phase rule).

## Flow & energy management

- **Timeboxing:** every activity has a visible time budget; a timer protects divergence from
  endless debate and signals respect for participants' time.
- **Parking lot (парковка):** a visible board for off-topic-but-valuable items so you can move on
  without dropping them; review it before close and assign owners.
- **Check-in / check-out:** a one-line round at the start (presence, expectation) and end (one
  takeaway) — cheap, raises inclusion and closes the loop.
- **Energizers / breaks:** short, purposeful — to re-engage a tired or stuck group; tie back to
  the metaphor method when you need a fresh angle, not just a stretch.

## Online & hybrid facilitation

- **Shared boards (Miro/FigJam/whiteboards):** the digital flipchart — make the main question and
  the scenario visible; use sticky-note moderation and dot-voting natively.
- **Breakout rooms:** the online equivalent of small-group work and World Café tables; brief a
  host per room, give a crisp prompt and a hard timebox, and have rooms report back.
- **Online World Café:** rotate participants across breakout rooms every ~20–25 min; a host stays
  to brief newcomers; collect each table's board into a shared canvas.
- **Pauses online are harder and more important:** silence feels longer on video — still hold it;
  use chat-storming (everyone types at once, then sends) to get parallel input and beat the
  one-speaker bottleneck. Default cameras-on norms early to build safety; watch for the quiet.

## International ↔ local synthesis

The strongest practice blends two sources of knowledge:
- **International standards & methodologies** — IAF (core competencies, handbook), and large
  multi-stakeholder dialogue practice (UN / OECD / World Bank "global dialogue" forums). These
  give a shared professional language and proven frameworks (World Café, Liberating Structures,
  SWOT/SOAR).
- **Local adaptation** — resources like facilab.ru contextualize these methods to the local
  culture and organization: adapted examples, local cases (corporate / public / startup),
  ready-made templates and checklists, and hybrid models combining Western structures with local
  practice.

Value is in the synthesis. Ignoring the international side → reinventing known solutions in
isolation. Ignoring the local side → blind copying that doesn't fit the real context. When
designing, anchor on a proven framework and translate it into the client's language, culture and
constraints.

## Scaling to global dialogue / community participation

At the largest level the facilitator becomes a *conductor*: success is measured less by the
decisions and more by whether the process was **inclusive, transparent and legitimate** to all
sides. Core moves: deliberately include voices usually unheard (give them real influence over
the decision, not just a survey); collaborative planning across sectors (government, business,
science, civil society); intense management of intercultural and political dynamics; high
patience and empathy. The same craft — process over content, sharp questions, careful design —
just at systemic scale.
