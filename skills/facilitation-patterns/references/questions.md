# Questions — the facilitator's primary instrument

Questions are what carry a group through the stages of thinking to a viable, inclusive decision.
They help the group hear the speaker's real meaning, gather full information, see the problem
from many sides; and they help the speaker shed the noise and notice their own assumptions.
A *right question* is the type of question that works best at a given moment, in a given
situation, with a given group — both a science and an art.

## The main question (главный вопрос)

The session's focusing question: open, agreed with the client in advance, posted visibly,
and sent in the invitation. Two parts:

**Main part (основная часть)** — assembles:
- the question word — usually **Что? / Как? / Какие?**
- **"we as who?"** — the role the group acts in (prevents role confusion)
- a **verb**
- the **period** (year/month or another marker, e.g. a revenue level) — when relevant
- the **place of effort** — dept / company / industry / region — when relevant
- the **rational goal**, ideally in the client's own words

**Motivational part (мотивационная часть)** — begins with **«Чтобы что…»** ("so that…"): the
effect on the wider context / stakeholders, or extra success criteria that raise the challenge.

### Verb = level of ownership
- **можем** — no constraints; idea generation; no responsibility for the result; wide frame.
- **следует** — some readiness to act; group not yet ready for concrete responsibility.
- **необходимо** — surface only the key items; group takes responsibility at least for the choice.
- **должно** — commit only to actions they'll voluntarily own; high responsibility.

Pick the verb to match the client's role (A/B/C) and the target pyramid level.

### Main-question checklist
- open;
- exploratory — makes the group think, allows multiple answers (not necessarily "creative");
- matches the pyramid level being targeted;
- its natural answer is the right **form**: need ideas → answers are ideas; need steps → steps;
  need scenarios → scenarios;
- uses the two-part construction (main part + «чтобы что…»);
- not overloaded with numbers/detail, but includes them when the task and common sense require;
- follows the client's context and is phrased in the client's language.

### Worked examples (two-part)
- Onboarding: «Что должно входить в процесс приёма и адаптации нового сотрудника, **чтобы** он
  познакомился с компанией и функционалом за максимально короткое время?»
- Project close: «Что необходимо сделать для завершения проекта, **чтобы** уложиться в срок и бюджет?»
- Strategy: «Что мы хотим видеть в результате наших активных действий через 2 года, **чтобы**
  стать первой компанией-дистрибьютором в городе N?»
- Values: «Какие ценности компании важно поддерживать, **чтобы** они работали на бренд
  работодателя в 20ХХ?»

## Strachan's six principles of asking

1. **Account for context** — group, experience, time, situation. A question that works for
   top-managers fails for novices. Check: what's the context? the goals/outcomes? what makes
   this group unique? what's comfortable/uncomfortable to discuss? how long have they worked
   together?
2. **Ask gently and with respect** — tone, volume, pace, face, posture, eye contact carry as
   much as words. Don't tower over the group; don't ask while turned to the flipchart; don't
   stare at one person when asking the whole group; ask permission before an emotionally loaded
   question; put the explanation before the term, not after, to avoid condescension.
3. **Don't lead** — facilitation isn't teaching; the question must not contain its answer. If
   you already know the answer, don't ask — just offer it (and tell the client). Make sure all
   viewpoints are heard: "Есть точки зрения, которые мы ещё не услышали? Кто смотрит иначе?"
4. **Stay neutral** — "accept, don't evaluate". No "great idea!"; ideas aren't good or bad.
   Thank people for *contributing*, not for the content. Be ready to hear anything without
   visible judgment. Stance: "every idea I hear is new — I choose to be curious about it".
5. **Keep the goal in focus** — questions relevant to the goal and the thinking stage; no closed
   questions during divergence, fine when wrapping up. Always know where the group is, was, and
   goes next. Understand *why* you ask each question.
6. **Listen and hold the pause** — a good facilitator doesn't ask many questions. After asking,
   the silence belongs to the group (20–40 sec; count to 10). Don't rephrase prematurely or
   pick on individuals — that steals their thinking time. Silence lets the group's wisdom surface.

## Self-check before asking a question
Unclear wording? Could it be read several ways? Does the answer depend on earlier questions
(so when is it best asked)? Does it need more context? Is it aimed at the outcomes we want?
Enough tension to spark good discussion? Does it touch something unique to the topic? What will
be hard for participants? Can they answer "I don't know"? Could different people interpret it
differently? What assumptions does it carry — do they fit the group? Why ask *this* group *this*
question *now*?

## Risk laddering
Clarify confidentiality and who'll be informed first. Start with low-risk questions (no strong
self-disclosure), build on success, then move to higher-stakes ones where opinions diverge.
Give people time to review materials before answering; allow anonymous formats (cards, dot/
multi-voting, envelopes) when useful. The key questions of the session must never be a surprise.

## A starter question bank by stage
- **Opening / framing:** "What would make this session worth your time?" · "What do we already
  agree on?"
- **Analysis:** "What's actually going on here?" · "What's the root cause behind the symptom?" ·
  "What are we *not* seeing?"
- **Divergence / options:** "How might it be if we had no constraints?" · "How is this solved
  elsewhere / in another industry / by people from the future?"
- **Convergence / decision:** "Against our criteria, which option holds up?" · "If it weren't
  your idea, which would you pick?" · "What would have to be true for us to commit?"
- **Roadmap:** "What's the first concrete step, by whom, by when?" · "What could derail this and
  how do we pre-empt it?"
- **Close / reflection:** "What shifted for you today?" · "What's the one thing you'll act on?"
