# Preparation, scenario & the after-phase

## Five preparation steps

**Step 1 — Define the goal.** State the group's goal per SMART; co-formulate with the client
which decisions the group must make. No clear goal → don't convene a facilitation.

**Step 2 — Participant selection.** Who has the needed experience/knowledge and needs to be
involved; fight both excess and shortage of participants.

**Step 3 — Prepare the inputs.** A short brief (situation → problem → goal); hand-out materials
with the facts needed to work. Accumulate info by interviewing the client, 1–2 future
participants, and 1–2 stakeholders (management / subordinates / adjacent units / clients /
competitors / contractors). In interviews capture: their view of the situation, proposed
solutions, and likely difficulties during the session. Then filter out noise, group info into
content classes, present it graphically (tables/flowcharts) where possible, and check it for
completeness and ease of perception.

**Step 4 — Determine the situation type.** Analysis / options / choice (see `methods.md`).

**Step 5 — Write the scenario** (see below).

## Scenario template

Build a detailed scenario as a grid:

| Содержание / Content | Результат / Result | Материалы / Materials | Время / Time |
|----------------------|--------------------|-----------------------|--------------|
| 1. Greet · 2. State the group's goal · 3. … | Participants understand the goal, are set to work | Slide with last-year overview | 10:00–10:10 (10 min) |
| … | … | … | … |
| Wrap up · restate how results are used · Q&A · thank | Participants understand next steps | — | 11:00–11:15 (15 min) |

Then:
- **Program-minimum / maximum:** what to cut if time runs short; what to add if there's slack.
- **Review the whole scenario:** it works toward the goal; activities are varied and alternate
  (whole group, pairs, small groups, presentation, sticker/dot voting); there's a min and a max
  variant; it's dynamically balanced; intellectual load is sensibly distributed.
- **Forecast difficulties** and plan preventive actions (below).

> The agent's deliverable uses a Markdown variant of this grid:
> `Этап | Время | Содержание/Вопросы | Механика | Визуализация`.

## Three phases on the day

1. **Opening:** clearly frame the task — goal of the activity, how work is organized, the form
   of the output, how results will be used, total time and per-activity time. Optionally a short
   motivating word from leadership / a content expert. Run an engaging warm-up into the problem.
2. **Running the activity:** hold the agreed format and regulations; push "deeper" (don't repeat;
   go from causes to causes of causes); concretize to a shared clear understanding; structure the
   output as you go.
3. **Closing:** summarize and restate the decision; assign (and secure) owners for the actions;
   restate how results will be used; thank participants; assign who will process/format the
   outputs and finish the detailed plan/document.

## Difficulty forecast — Head / Hands / Heart (Голова / Руки / Сердце)

| Area | Facilitator asks | Preventive actions |
|------|------------------|--------------------|
| **Knowledge — "Head"** | Have they met this topic? Enough expertise? Is terminology clear? Different meanings for the same term? Do they know group-decision rules? | Provide full info; select people with enough expertise; agree terms; agree ground rules; remind of the method used |
| **Skills — "Hands"** | Are group-work skills formed? A culture of interaction? Can they stay on goal? Enough analytical skill for the volume? | Return to the goal-setting frame, help phrasing (don't demand "perfection"); set clear rules, keep the regulations, park new topics on the flipchart; give simple analysis schemes, lower info complexity |
| **Motivation — "Heart"** | How does the topic touch their interests (what do they lose)? Attitude to the people/processes involved? Are they invested? Past experience with such sessions (old "grudges"?)? How much autonomy do they want? | Find out negatives in advance (losses, grudges, attitude to leadership); plan organizational moves that raise/lower the emotional field; make a "venting list" (лист слива); talk benefits first; don't deny losses — invite ways to minimize; explain how results will be used; pre-justify the chosen format and have alternatives |

## Handling disruptions live

| Disruption | Moves |
|------------|-------|
| **Passivity** | Ask questions, encourage; call by name; manage turn-taking |
| **Over-activity** | Thank for input, give others the floor; invite the quiet ones |
| **Argument** | Hear each side with understanding; stay neutral without bruising dignity; restate the goal; summarize |
| **Side-talk** | Ignore briefly; go silent; eye contact; ask the talkers a question (risky); invite them to share |
| **Latecomers** | Start on time and thank those present; don't put latecomers on the spot |

## The after-phase (mandatory)

Effectiveness doesn't end on the day. Capture and systematize all ideas and decisions; compile a
report; distribute it to participants and stakeholders; confirm owners and deadlines; ensure the
decisions are actually implemented. Without this, the work goes "в песок" (down the drain).
Always include a concrete after-phase plan in the session design.
